Audit Trail Risk Analytics Engine | SCTU

The Challenge: Clinical data coordinators spent 2–4 hours per reviewer per month manually filtering hundreds of thousands of rows of EDC audit trail exports in Excel, with no consistent methodology, no cross-site comparison, and no reproducibility. Risk indicators like after-hours edits, copy-paste patterns, and suspicious user-overwrite sequences went entirely undetected.

The Solution: Built a full-stack clinical risk-monitoring platform (Python extraction pipeline + R Shiny analytics engine) ingesting audit trail data directly from the Medidata RAVE RWS API. A schema normalisation layer resolves two divergent EDC CSV schemas into one canonical format at ingest time. Seven isolated analytical modules cover: excessive change rates, no-query edits, out-of-hours activity, copy-paste detection (time-window clustering across subject–field combinations), user-after-user sequence analysis, and a configurable multi-dimensional RAG risk scoring model across all sites simultaneously. Processes 1.7M+ records with a three-tier CSV reader (vroom → fread → read.csv fallback) and 250ms debounced reactive filters. One-click per-site Excel export via openxlsx.

The Impact (Production): Review time cut from 2–4 hours to under 10 minutes per trial (90–95% reduction) • 8–16 hours/month recovered across 2 reviewers • Standardised risk methodology eliminates reviewer-dependent variation entirely • 1,713,713 records ingested in ~3–5 seconds • All 5 originally requested risk dimensions covered plus a 6th synthesising Risk Summary • Zero data ever written or stored; fully read-only, GxP-adjacent.

R Shiny Python Medidata RAVE API Plotly GxP Compliance Risk Analytics openxlsx vroom

Peer-Reviewed Research

Applied machine learning research on energy forecasting, accepted at the UK AI Conference 2026.

Accepted · UK-AI 2026

An AI-Based Decision-Support Pipeline for Day-Ahead Photovoltaic Forecasting

Fariba Dehghan, Sebastian Stein, Vahid Yazdanpanah, Stephanie Gauthier, Masood Nazari

Proceedings of the UK AI Conference (UK-AI 2026) · arXiv:2608.02088 [cs.LG] · 13 pages, 6 figures

Newly deployed solar sites rarely have the long, clean histories that day-ahead forecasting models assume. Working from a UK charging-station site, we built a deployment-oriented pipeline that corrects timestamp conventions, constructs leakage-safe solar-geometry and clearness-index features, and combines complementary predictors through validation-learned stacking.

Results: 32% reduction in daylight normalised RMSE against a smart-persistence baseline under day-blocked evaluation (9% under the stricter rolling-origin protocol), and 6.6% / 6.4% improvements over the strongest individual machine learning baseline.

Machine Learning Ensemble Stacking Time Series Forecasting Feature Engineering Energy Systems
